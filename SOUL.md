# The soul of howitworks

What this project believes and how it sounds. CLAUDE.md says how to work;
this file says what good looks like. Read it before writing any user-facing
words — step copy, summaries, hooks, narration — or judging whether a scene
is finished.

## Who it's for

A curious person who has felt the machine — the fridge's hum, the warm grid
on its back, the click of a pen — but never seen inside. They are smart and
busy, not an engineer. We never talk down to them, and we never hide behind
jargon. Every explainer should end with them saying "*that's* what that
noise was."

## What we believe

- **The mechanism is sacred.** We teach the real physics of the real machine
  — researched, fact-checked, never simplified into being wrong. If the true
  mechanism is surprising, that surprise IS the content ("your fridge
  doesn't make cold"). We'd rather cut a step than fake one.
- **Show, don't assert.** The 3D scene is the argument; the words are the
  guide. Copy must describe what is literally on screen at that moment —
  copy-vs-visual truth is a defect class, not a nitpick.
- **Everything is built, nothing is imported.** Fully procedural models are
  a craft position, not just a technical one: we understand every part well
  enough to construct it, so the model can never lie about how the parts
  relate.
- **Premium is the floor.** The bar is a product shot: studio lighting, real
  materials, filleted edges, deliberate framing. A scene that looks like a
  programmer's demo isn't done — it should feel like the manufacturer's own
  cutaway film, made with more honesty.
- **Motion earns trust.** Seamless loops matter because a visible seam
  whispers "this is a trick." The machine should feel like it is genuinely
  running, indefinitely, in front of you.

## How we sound

- Concrete nouns, present tense, second person where it helps ("the black
  grid you've felt behind every fridge"). The reader's own experience is the
  entry point.
- One idea per step; each step earns the next. Explanations run cause →
  effect ("boiling drinks in heat"), never definition-first.
- Numbers are characters, not decoration: −26 °C, twenty years, a tube
  thinner than a hair. Pick the one number that makes the mechanism vivid;
  drop the rest.
- A little wonder, zero hype. We say "here is its secret," never "amazing"
  or "revolutionary." The mechanism supplies the awe; the prose stays calm.
- Hooks open a real question the mechanism will genuinely answer. No
  clickbait we can't cash.

## The finish line

An explainer is done when a stranger could scroll it once, understand the
machine, repeat the one surprising fact to a friend — and when every frame,
paused anywhere, would hold up as a still image we'd be proud to print.
